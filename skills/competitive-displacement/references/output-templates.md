# Output Templates: Competitive Displacement

## The displacement plan (produce this)

```
# Displacement Plan: [Account] vs [incumbent / competitor]

## Incumbent read
- Strong at: [what the buyer genuinely gets today]
- Falls short for this buyer: [the specific gap this account feels]
- Cost of switching (the real opponent): [migration, retraining, political cost]

## Differentiation (approach and outcome, not features)
1. [Difference in how you work or what they end up with] - why it matters to this buyer.
2. [...]
3. [...]
The villain: [the status-quo problem the incumbent structurally cannot solve].

## Switching story (why now)
[What has changed in their business or market that makes standing still the risky choice.
Dissatisfaction + vision + an easy first step, against the resistance to change.]

## Trap-setting questions (surface the gaps, no badmouthing)
1. What must be true of a solution for this to actually work for you?
2. [Question that surfaces a frustration with the current approach, neutrally.]
3. [Question that raises a nonobvious risk they have not considered.]

## Open gaps
- [MISSING: ...] e.g. incumbent unknown, or switching cost not yet understood
```

## Notes

- Lead with the approach, not the product. Win the criteria and the comparison shrinks.
- Keep the differences to approach and outcome. A feature list drags you back into the bake-off.
- Let the buyer name the incumbent's gaps. Ask, do not accuse.
- No em dashes, no jargon.
