# Frameworks: QBR Prep

## Value tied to their goals

Value is not what your product did. Value is what their business got, measured against the goal
they bought for. So the value section is built backward from the original success criteria:
- What was the problem statement that drove the purchase? (Despite trying [X], they could not
  [outcome] because of [problem], costing [amount].)
- What success criteria did they set, and what were the target metrics?
- For each win this quarter, draw the line back to one of those goals, in their numbers.

A win the customer cannot connect to a goal they care about lands as a feature, not value. If
you cannot tie it to a goal, either find the goal or drop the win from the headline.

## Risk signals

Go in having named the risks yourself. The ones that matter:
- **Engagement dropping.** Fewer logins, a champion who has gone quiet, meetings getting
  rescheduled. Behavior, not vibe.
- **Missed goals.** A success criterion that is not on track. Name it and bring the plan.
- **A leadership change.** A new executive over your champion can reset the whole relationship.
- **A competitive threat.** Signs they are evaluating an alternative, or a renewal at risk.

For each risk, bring the evidence and a plan. A risk you raise first, with a plan attached, is a
sign of a partner. A risk the customer raises first is a fire drill.

## Expansion

Expansion is the natural next outcome, not an upsell. Find it in three places:
- **New use cases:** a next problem your product already solves that they have not turned on.
- **Adjacent teams:** a team next to your champion with the same problem, reachable by a warm
  intro built on a proven outcome.
- **Usage signals:** adoption or outcome data that points to a real opening.

Rank by impact and ease of entry. The strongest opening is usually a new executive with a new
mandate, or a proven result you can extend to the next team. Frame every expansion as their next
outcome, tied to a goal, so it opens a conversation instead of triggering a guard.

## Lead with their success

The agenda order matters. Open with what they achieved, in their metrics. Address risk in the
middle, proactively, with a plan. Open expansion last, as the next outcome. The moment a QBR
feels like a sales pitch, the customer stops treating you as a partner and starts treating you as
a vendor to manage. Lead with them.
