# The Philosophy

These skills are the productized version of the system in *Selling With* and *Brief & Brilliant*. Understanding the ideas underneath them is the difference between running the plays and running them well.

## Selling with, not selling to

Enterprise buying decisions get made in internal meetings you are not invited to. So the rep's job is not to pitch and close. It is to enable a champion who carries the narrative when you are not in the room. Buyers close deals, not reps. No champion, no deal. Every skill in this set is built around that reality.

## The buyer's language wins

The fastest way to get ignored is to sound like a vendor. The fastest way to get forwarded is to sound like the buyer. So the skills mine your call transcripts and emails for the exact words the buyer used, and write with those.

The rule is **camouflage, not a billboard**. A great deal artifact looks like it was written inside the buyer's own company, in their words, about their priority. When you swap their language for polished vendor vocabulary, the champion stops recognizing their own world in it, and it stops traveling.

## A champion has the three I's

Not everyone friendly to you is a champion. A real champion has all three:

- **Incentive** — they personally win when the deal closes.
- **Influence** — people inside actually listen to them on this decision.
- **Information** — they know how the buying really works, and where it stalls.

Incentive and information without influence is a coach. Influence and information without incentive is someone who will not spend capital for you. The skills qualify the person before they arm them.

## Connect every stakeholder to a priority they own

A problem only gets funded when it blocks a priority someone already owns. The skills chain each stakeholder's pain to the metric above them with the connector "which means": this user's daily friction, which means this manager's number, which means this executive's KPI. That chain is what lets one message speak to a whole committee.

## Make it forwardable

The test of any deal artifact is whether your champion can forward it without editing, and have it land with someone who was not on the call. That is why the skills produce one-page business cases, four-sentence soundbites, and per-stakeholder emails built to travel. If a sentence would survive being pasted into a different deal, it is too generic to send.

## Math problems versus drama

When a deal stalls, name what is actually blocking it. A solvable problem has a next step: a missing stakeholder, no business case, an unvalidated number. Drama has no next step, because it is protecting something (a job, a past decision, a turf line). A better deck does not move drama. Confusing the two is how deals sit in a forecast for two quarters. The skills force the distinction.

## The operator's read

The last thing every skill does is add the part a summary cannot: the one thing an experienced operator would notice that the data does not show. The stakeholder who matters more than their title. The decider who was not in the room. The strength that is quietly a trap. Anyone can produce the map. The read is the judgment.
